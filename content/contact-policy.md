I get a fair amount of email, and I want to be upfront about how I handle it so that you don't spend time writing something that I can't respond to well. This is a draft of my policy, and I'll update it as things change.

My email is kmisra \[at\] utexas \[dot\] edu.

## If you are interested in applying to the PhD program

I am looking to recruit PhD students through the [Linguistics Department at UT Austin](https://liberalarts.utexas.edu/linguistics/), expected to start in Fall 2027. I am primarily interested in working with students who have interests at the intersection of AI <u>and</u> CogSci/Ling. Here is what you should know before reaching out:

- **Applications:** Admissions are handled by the department's admissions committee, and you can read about the process [here](https://liberalarts.utexas.edu/linguistics/graduate-programs/how-to-apply.html). Emailing me does not affect your application in any way (positively or negatively), so please don't feel like you have to.
- **I will probably not reply to emails asking about admission chances, or whether I am taking students.** The answer to the second one is on my [homepage](/) and it will be updated there. I am unable to evaluate your chances from a short email, and the committee looks at the full application anyway.
- **If you do email me, make sure it shows you've looked at my website.** Tell me what about my research (or my group's) interests you, and what you'd want to work on. Generic emails that could've been sent to any professor will unfortunately not get a response. Please include your CV, and keep it short!
- **In your application,** mention me (and anyone else in the department) in your statement of purpose if you'd like to work with them. That is the best way for me to find out you are interested.

## If you are a current UT student

Please come to my office hours, or email me! I'm happy to talk about research, classes, grad school applications, and so on. If you're in one of my classes, check the syllabus first, since it will likely have the answer to your question.

## If you are interested in research opportunities (MS, undergraduate, interns, visitors)

Unfortunately, I am not able to take on remote interns or visiting students at the moment. If you're a UT student, see above. Otherwise, I will likely not be able to respond.

## If you have a question about my work

Questions about papers, [minicons](https://kanishka.website/minicons), or code are very welcome! If it's about minicons, opening an issue on GitHub is usually the fastest option, since others can benefit from the answer too. For papers, please do email me.

## If you are inviting me to review, give a talk, or serve on a committee

Thank you for thinking of me! I will try to respond within a week or two. I may have to decline for the sake of balancing my workload, but I will always try to point you to others who might be a good fit.

## A few general notes

- I do not always respond to every email, and I may take some time to do so (I'd say a week or two is typical). A nudge after two weeks is totally fine.
- Please don't send me the same email multiple times, or to multiple addresses of mine.
- I will not respond to unsolicited emails that look like mass mailings, or that have clearly not read this page.
- **On AI-written emails:** please write to me in your own words. I'm a researcher who works with language models, so I'm not against them in general, but an email that was clearly generated (generic praise of my work, a summary of my papers that I didn't need, nothing specific about *you*) tells me very little about who is writing it. I will most likely not respond to those. Using a tool to fix your grammar or phrasing is totally fine, especially if English isn't your first language. What I want to read is your own interests and ideas, even if the writing is imperfect.
